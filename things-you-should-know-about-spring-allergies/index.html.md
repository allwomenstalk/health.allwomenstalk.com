# 7 Things You Should Know About Managing Spring Allergies

Spring should be a time to celebrate the arrival of warmer weather and blooming greenery, but for many of us, it signals the start of a challenging season: spring allergies. While most people eagerly look forward to spring, those who suffer from seasonal allergies often find themselves wishing winter would last just a bit longer. Having developed spring allergies as an adult, I compiled a practical list of insights I wish I had known when my symptoms first appeared.

## 1 They Can Come on at Any Time

One of the biggest surprises about spring allergies that I discovered is that they can spring up at any time. Up until three years ago, I never had the slightest reaction to pollen, but for some reason, I developed a strong reaction to it that seemingly came out of nowhere. So if you start to notice that you have a perpetual cold every spring, you may want to consult your doctor to see if you have developed seasonal allergies, even if you never had them before. If you suspect your body is reacting to seasonal shifts, you can also explore [allergy testing options](#3) to pinpoint your exact triggers.

## 2 You Can Be Allergic to More than Just Flowers

Most people assume that they are allergic to flowers and weeds in the spring, but there are many other triggers to consider. When I first went to an allergy specialist, he explained that there are four main categories of allergens for those with cold-like symptoms: plants and weeds, animals, mold, and trees. Turns out, many people with spring allergies are actually allergic to tree pollen rather than flowers.

- Tree pollen (such as birch, oak, and cedar)
- Grass and weed pollen
- Outdoor mold spores found in damp soil and fallen leaves
- Pet dander and dust mites exacerbated by seasonal changes

According to resource materials from the [Asthma and Allergy Foundation of America](https://www.aafa.org/), tree pollen is actually one of the earliest and most widespread triggers each spring.

## 3 You Can Get Tested to See Exactly What You're Allergic to

As mentioned earlier, there are four main categories of allergens you can get tested for if you are experiencing persistent cold-like symptoms: plants/weeds, animals, mold, and trees. Typically, an allergist will perform a skin prick test using common environmental allergens prevalent in your geographic region. If the area where your skin is pricked becomes raised, red, or itchy, it indicates a reaction to that specific allergen. Identifying your triggers helps you take proactive steps to avoid exposure whenever possible.

| Allergen Category | Primary Triggers | Common Symptoms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tree Pollen | Birch, Oak, Maple | Sneezing, watery eyes |
| Weed & Grass | Ragweed, Timothy Grass | Congestion, itchy throat |
| Mold Spores | Damp soil, fallen leaves | Coughing, sinus pressure |

Knowing your specific sensitivities enables you to tailor your daily routine and treatment plan effectively.

## 4 You Should Start Your Medication before You Get Symptoms

One of the biggest mistakes allergy sufferers make is waiting to take medication until after symptoms start. If you begin taking antihistamines or nasal sprays two to three weeks before allergy season ramps up, you can prevent histamine build-up and significantly reduce your symptoms. Pharmacists and health professionals at the [American College of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology](https://acaai.org/) recommend tracking local pollen counts so you know precisely when to begin your regimen. If standard over-the-counter options aren't providing adequate relief, talk with your doctor about long-term treatments like [desensitization shots](#5).

## 5 There Are Desensitization Shots Available

While there is no instant cure for seasonal allergies, effective strategies exist to keep symptoms manageable. Standard treatments like prescription nasal sprays and eye drops provide temporary relief. However, if medication offers only marginal improvement, immunotherapy—commonly known as allergy shots—presents a valuable alternative. Learn more about how immunotherapy works through [Mayo Clinic's guide to allergy shots](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/allergies/in-depth/allergy-shots/art-20044943). These treatments gradually expose your immune system to small doses of your specific allergens over time, helping your body build lasting tolerance and immunity. Don't lose hope if standard pills fall short, as advanced medical options can provide long-term comfort.

## 6 Don't Rub Your Eyes!

While individual reactions to pollen vary, itchy and irritated eyes rank among the most uncomfortable symptoms. When your eyes feel intensely itchy, resist the urge to rub them. Rubbing transfers additional pollen from your hands into your eyes, aggravating inflammation and worsening symptoms. Instead, rinse your eyes with sterile saline solution and apply lubricating or anti-itch eye drops. If you frequently experience severe ocular discomfort, coordinate with your specialist while maintaining proper steps for [reducing indoor allergen exposure](#7).

## 7 Staying inside Helps

Ultimately, as hard as it can be to stay indoors during pleasant spring weather, limiting outdoor exposure during high pollen counts is one of the most reliable ways to keep symptoms under control. Keep your home's windows closed to prevent airborne pollen from entering your living space.

- Keep windows closed during peak pollen hours (usually morning and afternoon)
- Use high-efficiency HEPA air filters in your home HVAC system
- Wipe down clothing and pet fur after spending time outdoors

Spring allergies can certainly be a nuisance, but taking proactive steps makes the season far more enjoyable. What strategies or habits have helped you manage your spring allergies?
