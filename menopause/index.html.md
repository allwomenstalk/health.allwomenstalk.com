# Menopause Treatment: Hormone Therapy and Other Options ...

Hot flashes, night sweats and disrupted sleep can start during perimenopause, years before periods stop. Treatment choices include hormone therapy, nonhormonal prescription medicines and some behavioral approaches. The right choice depends on your symptoms, medical history and preferences. You can ask for help without waiting until symptoms become unbearable.

## What to discuss about hormone therapy

[ACOG describes systemic hormone therapy](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/hormone-therapy-for-menopause) as the most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats. Whether it is appropriate for you depends on factors such as your age, time since menopause, personal history and risks of blood clots, stroke, heart disease and certain cancers. Review these with an ob-gyn or another qualified clinician instead of using a single rule for everyone.

The type of hormone therapy matters. If you still have a uterus, systemic estrogen is generally paired with a progestin to lower the risk of uterine cancer. If you have had a hysterectomy, estrogen alone may be an option. ACOG also distinguishes systemic treatment from low-dose local vaginal estrogen for vaginal symptoms. Your clinician can explain which formulation and route fit your situation.

## If hormones are not right for you

There are prescription options for hot flashes that do not contain estrogen, including certain antidepressants and newer neurokinin receptor antagonists. The choice depends on your symptoms, other medicines and health history. Ask which benefits, adverse effects, monitoring and follow-up apply before starting a medicine.

For vaginal dryness or pain with sex, ask specifically about local treatment options. These symptoms deserve care, even if you are not taking systemic hormone therapy.

## Can non-drug approaches help?

Behavioral approaches can be part of a treatment plan, especially when sleep and stress are affected. Ask your clinician which options have evidence for your particular symptoms and what to expect from them. They need not replace effective medical treatment if you want it.

## What about black cohosh and other supplements?

ACOG [does not recommend plant and herbal remedies for hot flashes](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/experts-and-stories/ask-acog/what-can-i-do-to-help-with-hot-flashes), including black cohosh. Supplements can have side effects and interact with other medicines. Tell your clinician what you take, including products sold as “natural.” Avoid assuming that a supplement is proven or safe because it is widely sold.

## Questions for your appointment

- Could my symptoms be related to perimenopause or menopause, or should we check for another cause?

- Am I a candidate for hormone therapy, and which type and route would fit my history?

- Which nonhormonal prescription options are suitable for me?

- What can help with sleep, mood or vaginal symptoms?

- How will we know whether treatment is working, and when should we review it?

Menopause care also includes prevention. Ask about bone health and which screenings are due. Our [heart-health guide](https://health.allwomenstalk.com/things-every-woman-needs-to-know-about-heart-disease/) can help you prepare questions about cardiovascular risk.

## FAQ

### Is hormone therapy safe for everyone?

No treatment suits everyone. A clinician should weigh your symptoms and personal risks with you. ACOG lists conditions for which systemic hormone therapy is usually not recommended, including a history of blood clots, stroke, heart attack or certain cancers.

### Do I need progesterone if I take estrogen?

If you still have a uterus, systemic estrogen is generally paired with a progestin to protect the uterine lining. After a hysterectomy, estrogen alone may be an option. Ask which type applies to you.

### What can I try if I do not want hormones?

Ask about evidence-based nonhormonal prescription medicines and approaches for the symptoms that bother you. The best choice depends on your health history and goals.

### Does black cohosh work for hot flashes?

ACOG does not recommend plant and herbal remedies such as black cohosh for hot flashes. Discuss any supplement you are considering with your clinician.
