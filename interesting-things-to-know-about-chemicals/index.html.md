# 7 Important Things to Know About Everyday Chemicals

For anyone interested in their health and the environment, understanding how daily chemicals impact your wellness is essential. The long-term effects of synthetic chemicals and environmental toxins remain a major focus of scientific research, particularly regarding their impact on human health, wildlife, and ecosystems. Making informed choices for a cleaner, greener lifestyle can feel overwhelming, but staying knowledgeable about common exposures helps you make empowered decisions for yourself and your family.

## 1. Number of Commonly Used Chemicals

Navigating daily product ingredients can feel like a minefield when making healthy choices. More than 84,000 chemicals are registered and approved for commercial use by federal regulatory bodies like the [U.S. Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/). These substances are widely present in daily items including skincare, hair care, pharmaceuticals, plastics, food packaging, textiles, dyes, paints, and household cleaners. With such a vast array of synthetic compounds featuring hard-to-pronounce names on product labels, standing in the beauty aisle trying to choose non-toxic options can certainly feel confusing.

Common categories where these commercial chemicals are frequently found include:

- Personal care products, cosmetics, and toiletries
- Household cleaners, detergents, and surface disinfectants
- Processed food additives, preservatives, and food dyes

## 2. Synthetic Chemicals Used in Cosmetics

Synthetic chemicals serve as primary functional ingredients in manufacturing modern cosmetics and personal care items. Over 10,500 individual chemical ingredients are utilized in cosmetic formulations. These include industrial-grade chemical classes such as degreasers, plasticizers, and surfactants. While many of these raw components are also utilized in industrial applications, auto manufacturing, and commercial paints, they frequently end up in the formulations applied directly onto our skin, face, and bodies every day.

Understanding these ingredient lists helps when evaluating [chemicals in personal care items](#4) that you use daily.

## 3. Percentage of Approved Chemicals

While knowing that over 84,000 commercial chemicals exist is striking, a broader concern is that only a tiny fraction—approximately 1%—have undergone comprehensive health and safety testing. (Ref: [edition.cnn.com](https://edition.cnn.com)) Regulatory oversight often lags behind chemical production, meaning it can take decades of consumer feedback, adverse event reporting, and clinical research before a dangerous substance is officially restricted or removed from commercial approved lists.

## 4. Number of Chemicals in Personal Care Items

Synthetic compounds are embedded in most routine personal care products unless explicitly formulated with verified natural ingredients. Studies by health research organizations show that the average adult uses 9 personal care products daily, exposing themselves to roughly 126 unique chemical ingredients before leaving home. (Ref: [ewg.org](https://www.ewg.org/news-insights/news/2004/12/exposures-add-survey-results)) A typical morning routine involving body wash, antiperspirant, cleanser, toner, moisturizer, shampoo, conditioner, styling product, and toothpaste accumulates a substantial cumulative chemical load. Adding extra products like sunscreen, lip balm, body lotion, and hand cream increases this overall daily exposure.

This daily accumulation highlights why evaluating [synthetic chemicals used in cosmetics](#2) is such an essential step toward cleaner living.

| Skincare & Haircare | 4-6 products | ~60-80 ingredients |

## 5. Chemicals in Food

Chemical additives are also widespread throughout our food supply, with more than 3,000 chemical substances permitted on the FDA list of allowed food additives. Common additives include well-known compounds like monosodium glutamate (MSG), trans fats, and sodium nitrate, along with lesser-known preservatives like potassium bromate. Nutrition researchers and health advocates frequently express concern over how continuous exposure to these combined dietary additives may affect long-term health and metabolic function.

## 6. The Total Quantity of Pesticides Used in the U.S

Agricultural chemical use represents another significant area of exposure. In the United States alone, over 5.1 billion pounds of pesticides are applied annually to crops and agricultural land. Pesticide formulations are designed to control pests, but many contain neurotoxic compounds capable of disrupting endocrine systems, disturbing hormonal balance, and increasing long-term health risks. For consumers who cannot consistently purchase organic produce, prioritizing thorough washing and choosing fruits and vegetables with lower pesticide residue levels can help mitigate risk.

## 7. Water Systems Polluted with Chemicals

Water quality is another critical area where chemical runoff and treatment byproducts impact daily health. According to environmental research, over 100 million people in the United States rely on municipal water systems containing measurable levels of industrial contaminants and disinfection byproducts. (Ref: [ewg.org](https://www.ewg.org/research/water-treatment-contaminants)) For example, municipal water treated with chlorine can form disinfection byproducts known as trihalomethanes (THMs), which have been linked to elevated health risks over prolonged exposure. Using certified water filtration systems at home offers an effective, accessible way to significantly reduce these tap water contaminants.

Official resources from environmental organizations like the [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency](https://www.epa.gov/) offer guidelines on testing drinking water and installing appropriate filtration systems.

Combining clean water filtration with aware choices regarding [chemicals in food](#5) helps create a well-rounded approach to reducing daily environmental toxin exposure.

While chemical research continues to evolve, taking proactive steps to understand your everyday exposures allows you to make informed decisions for your household. Staying educated, reading product labels, and choosing cleaner alternatives where possible empowers you to protect your long-term health. How do you feel about chemical exposure in daily life—do you actively seek out non-toxic alternatives, or do you take a balanced approach?
