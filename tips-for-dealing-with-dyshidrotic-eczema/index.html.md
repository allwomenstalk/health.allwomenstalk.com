# 7 Essential Tips for Dealing with Dyshidrotic Eczema

Do you need some practical tips for dealing with dyshidrotic eczema? This specific type of eczema primarily affects the hands (and occasionally the feet), causing intensely itchy, painful little blisters or "bubbles." Because our hands come into daily contact with endless soaps, chemicals, and environmental triggers, managing flare-ups can feel overwhelming. Having been diagnosed with it myself, I have spent years figuring out what truly helps calm outbreaks and protect fragile skin. According to the [National Eczema Association](https://nationaleczema.org/eczema/types-of-eczema/dyshidrotic-eczema/), identifying personal triggers is one of the most effective ways to manage symptoms. Here are seven tried-and-tested tips for dealing with dyshidrotic eczema.

## 1. Have Allergy Tests

The first of my tips for dealing with dyshidrotic eczema is to work out what specific substances trigger your symptoms. The most reliable way to do this is to undergo formal allergy testing by asking your primary care doctor for a referral to a dermatologist. While patch testing may not pinpoint every single potential irritant in your environment, knowing your primary triggers gives you a clear starting point for avoiding daily exposure.

## 2. Check Labels

Once you know what triggers your reactions, start reading product labels very carefully. While checking ingredient lists on every bottle takes extra time, it is far better than dealing with an uncomfortable eczema outbreak. Preservatives like Methylisothiazolinone (MI) and Methylchloroisothiazolinone (MCI) are notorious triggers frequently found in household cleaners, dish soaps, and shampoos. Reviewing official [dermatology guidelines](https://www.aad.org/public/diseases/eczema/types/dyshidrotic-eczema) can help you spot hidden irritants before they touch your skin. Reading labels carefully will also help you avoid unnecessary flare-ups when [treating an active outbreak](#3).

## 3. Treating an Outbreak

Start addressing a flare-up the moment you notice initial symptoms. Over-the-counter hydrocortisone or prescribed steroid creams can help reduce acute inflammation quickly, though they should be used sparingly to avoid skin thinning. I have found great success with a structured three-step recovery routine:

- Apply hydrocortisone cream during initial redness and blistering.
- Switch to a soothing herbal ointment once skin cracks heal.
- Finish with a rich moisturizer or body butter to restore the skin barrier.

## 4. Protect Your Hands

Protecting your hands from harsh chemicals and household detergents is essential for everyone, but it is critical if you suffer from dyshidrotic eczema or contact dermatitis. Always wear protective rubber or nitrile gloves lined with cotton when washing dishes, handling cleaning solutions, washing the car, or doing household chores. Minimizing direct contact with cleaning agents prevents sudden flare-ups and keeps skin barriers intact.

## 5. Relieve Itching

One of the most challenging aspects of dyshidrotic eczema is the intense, persistent itch. While doctors recommend avoiding scratching to prevent infection, holding back is easier said than done! A soothing natural salve can significantly reduce irritation. I like using a gentle herbal ointment containing calendula or chamomile from a local health food store. Alternatively, creating a simple paste using baking soda and cold water offers immediate cooling relief to calm severe itching before moving on to your [daily moisturizing routine](#6).

## 6. Keep Moisturising

Consistent hydration is essential for healing eczema-prone skin. I have noticed that recovery takes significantly longer whenever I skip regular moisturizing, as dry skin cracks easily and becomes susceptible to further irritation. Look for rich, fragrance-free ointments or thick body butters that your skin tolerates well, and reapply throughout the day—especially after washing your hands. You can also make a custom moisturizer using raw shea butter blended with vitamin E or jojoba oil.

## 7. Stress

Emotional and physical stress is a well-documented trigger for hand eczema flare-ups, making stress management an important part of your skin care routine. Because the skin is our largest organ, it frequently reflects internal stress and anxiety. Medical research highlighted by the [Mayo Clinic](https://www.mayoclinic.org/diseases-conditions/dyshidrotic-eczema/symptoms-causes/syc-20352356) underscores the connection between stress and skin flare-ups. Incorporating relaxation practices like yoga, deep breathing exercises, or mindfulness can help reduce outbreak frequency. Ultimately, getting an [official allergy test](#1) and managing your stress level go hand-in-hand for long-term comfort.

Avoiding everyday topical triggers can be challenging because new sensitivities can develop over time, even after allergy testing. Furthermore, certain ingredients like sodium lauryl sulfate (SLS) can irritate delicate skin even without a formal allergy. Opting for gentle, organic products or crafting homemade alternatives ensures full control over what touches your hands.
