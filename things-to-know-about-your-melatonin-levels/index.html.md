# 7 Things to Know About Your Melatonin Levels for Better Sleep

Your melatonin level may not be the first thing you consider when working toward better overall health, but understanding how this hormone functions is essential for quality rest. Melatonin is a key hormone for regulating a healthy sleep cycle, and it also plays a significant role in your entire body's vitality and sense of daily well-being. Explore these essential insights below, and prepare to get a restorative night's rest tonight!

## 1. Easily Disrupted

Our melatonin levels naturally increase at sunset to help our bodies prepare for rest, but everyday factors can easily offset this natural trigger. Exposure to blue light from screens, stress, and even daylight saving time can suppress melatonin release when you need it most. According to [Mayo Clinic sleep hygiene tips](https://www.mayoclinic.org/healthy-lifestyle/adult-health/in-depth/sleep/art-20048379), creating a calming bedtime wind-down routine helps signal to your body that it is time to sleep.

To protect your natural evening melatonin signal, try these simple habits:

- Power down computers, smartphones, and televisions 30 to 60 minutes before bed
- Engage in soothing activities like reading, journaling, or listening to quiet music
- Keep bedroom lighting dim once the sun goes down

## 2. Sunrise is Best

Melatonin levels function best when our daily sleep schedules align with natural daylight patterns. Heading to bed by 10 p.m. and waking around 6 a.m. supports natural circadian rhythms and optimizes melatonin production. Aligning your sleep-wake schedule with the sun can create a dramatic positive shift in both your sleep quality and daytime energy levels.

## 3. Weight Control

Staying awake late into the night often triggers late-night snacking and cravings for high-calorie junk foods past 11 p.m. This habit not only adds excess calories but can also disrupt metabolic signals and cause daytime sugar cravings for quick energy. Aim for a consistent bedtime by 10 p.m. or 11 p.m. at the latest. Along with avoiding late-night kitchen raids, incorporating [melatonin-rich foods](#7) into your dinner or light evening snack can naturally support your rest.

## 4. Depression

Melatonin is one of the crucial hormones influencing the neurochemical balance necessary for supporting mood and emotional well-being. It works alongside other vital neurotransmitters like serotonin, which plays a key role in mood regulation. As highlighted by [Sleep Foundation research](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/melatonin), maintaining healthy melatonin cycles contributes significantly to emotional resilience and overall mental wellness.

## 5. Declines with Age

Natural production of many key hormones, including melatonin, declines as we age. This decline makes prioritizing consistent sleep hygiene more important than ever. Establishing a regular schedule and turning in early can help offset age-related drops in melatonin, leaving you feeling refreshed, energized, and rejuvenated each morning. If age-related sleep changes persist, learn how to [supplement with care](#6) to support your sleep routine safely.

## 6. Supplement with Care

If you choose to take a melatonin supplement, approach it with thoughtful guidance. Over-the-counter melatonin can be helpful for short-term adjustments, such as overcoming jet lag or resetting sleep schedules over a few weeks. However, long-term or high-dose usage may lead to unwanted side effects like morning drowsiness, vivid dreams, headaches, or dizziness. Review [NCCIH guidance on melatonin supplements](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know) and consult your doctor or pharmacist to choose a reputable brand and proper dosage.

## 7. Food Tips

You can support your body's melatonin production naturally through diet. Foods rich in melatonin, tryptophan, magnesium, and essential vitamins provide the building blocks your body needs for sound sleep. In addition to dietary adjustments, ensure your overall environment is free from [disruptions to your body clock](#1).

| Food Category | Top Examples | Key Nutrients |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fruits | Tart cherries, Bananas | Natural Melatonin, Potassium |
| Nuts & Seeds | Walnuts, Chia seeds, Pumpkin seeds | Tryptophan, Magnesium |
| Vegetables & Grains | Spinach, Oatmeal, Sweet potatoes | Vitamin B6, Complex Carbs |

In addition to eating nutrient-dense foods like poultry, eggs, almonds, and leafy greens, avoid consuming caffeine, heavy meals, spicy foods, sugary treats, and alcohol close to bedtime, as these can severely disrupt sleep quality.

Prioritizing consistent sleep is a fundamental pillar of overall health. Factors like jet lag, late social outings, stress, and late-evening workouts can all disrupt your natural melatonin production. By maintaining a steady sleep routine, your body and melatonin levels will benefit immensely.
